Lecture 7: Knowledge Representation
===================================

How do we store what a program "knows" so it can reason with it?

Fact-based Representation
-------------------------
The simplest approach: facts as simple statements.
"is_a(sparrow, bird)", "can_fly(bird)". New facts can follow
from old ones: if a sparrow is a bird and birds can fly,
a sparrow can fly. This chaining of facts is called inference.

Retrieval vs Reasoning
----------------------
Two different problems: FINDING the stored information that
matches a question (retrieval), and DERIVING new information
from stored facts (reasoning). Modern search engines are
retrieval systems. Expert systems in the 1980s tried
reasoning and struggled with scale.

Databases vs Documents
----------------------
Structured data (tables, key-value pairs) answers precise
queries efficiently. Unstructured text (notes, articles)
requires ranking methods like TF-IDF to decide which document
is most relevant to a query. Weighting terms by how rare they
are across a collection turns text search into scoring.
